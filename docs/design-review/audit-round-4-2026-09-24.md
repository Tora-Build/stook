# Audit round 4: the whole product (2026-09-24)

Seven blind lenses (program security, algorithm, series and oracle, keeper and infrastructure, site and Worker, app correctness, docs) over HEAD, each finding attacked by two skeptics. 64 agents, none failed; nothing refuted.

## Closing memo

**Bottom line:** I found no way to steal funds, and the maths pays out correctly. The real problems are elsewhere: anyone can steer how a new coin's series learns its volatility, the app crashes or fails in a few places, the keeper wastes SOL, and a public GET request can crash the price service. None of this needs a halt, but items 1 to 6 below should be fixed before mainnet.

**1. Security and payouts:** These checks all held: account binding, signers, PDAs, grace-period payouts (they only reach the owner), the Token-2022 fee harvest, Pyth reads and integer overflow. The code is in `instructions/ladder.rs`, `instructions/series.rs` and `oracle.rs`. A day cannot be re-created after it closes. In a void, depositors are paid first and claims come out the same in any order. Measured compute fits every path except the app's Collect buttons.

**2. Algorithm:** It is sound. Across 300 fuzzed rounds and 60,000 trades:
- the house never ran short of cash, and redemptions summed to payouts exactly
- tranche terms matched within 3 wei
- the fee ramp and the SDK's copies of the program maths matched with zero mismatches

The real weakness is in `series_observe` (`state/series.rs`): anyone can choose which daily closes a series learns from. On a new series, that can leave the learned volatility at 0.3 to 0.4 of the honest value, costing the house (LPs) roughly 10 to 23% of their deposit per round for the first weeks. It can also delay warm-up by about 19 closes. Smaller issues:
- `ladder_open` does not wait for yesterday's close to be learned
- weekday series scale by calendar time, about 0.93x
- settlement learns from closes with confidence up to 10%, while observe requires 1%
- after the 24h void grace, a round that could still settle can be voided
- in a void, a self-dealing pair of wallets can take up to half of the other traders' refund (the house is untouched)

**3. Website and infra:**
- Any visitor can crash the tape service with `coin=__proto__` or `constructor` (`infra/tape`, `site/worker.js`).
- The site has no CSP, frame protection or HSTS, and the Worker serves the tape's responses unchanged.
- `/tape/register` accepts any caller if TAPE_TOKEN is unset.
- `infra/rpc-proxy` is an open relay for the keyed Alchemy endpoint.
- The public devnet faucet key also holds the coins' transfer-fee and withheld-fee withdraw authority.
- The keeper (`infra/ladder-crank/src/index.mjs`) pays rent for token accounts it creates for strangers, which anyone can farm as a SOL drain.
- With FULL_VERIFICATION=1 (the mainnet setting), every keeper post leaves an encoded-VAA account's rent unclaimed.
- For a round whose series has not warmed up, the keeper reposts a Pyth update every few seconds all day.

**4. Docs:** The core numbers match the code. Stale or wrong:
- `docs/architecture.md`: it still describes per-band LP attribution and a creator fee, line 287 lists transfer fees as Refused, lines 148-150 say a missed close can always be backfilled, and lines 99-100 say a losing trader never prefers a void
- the README Status section, the programs-core README's counts, the app README's routes, the backtest README and LAUNCH-KIT
- `How.tsx:144` pays LPs 80% of fees while its text says 90%
- `How.tsx:54` misstates where the settlement price comes from

**5. Must fix, in order:**
1. Bound warm-up: fix the starting close at `series_create`, require consecutive closes (allow one skip), and allow later skips only after a delay.
2. Collect budgets: `Ticket.tsx:189` and `Yours.tsx:138` budget 80K, but claiming a deposit made before open costs about 114K. Budget each item by kind and add an SDK claimComputeUnits helper.

6. Faucet key: remove its fee and withdraw authorities.

## Verified findings

### Permissionless series_observe lets anyone choose which closes a series learns from: a cheap attack blocks the warm-up, and a backfill can steer the variance

*program-security · security · reviewer medium, verifiers medium, medium · day*

series_observe accepts any index whose close is later than last_at. It does not require the next day, and it lets a fresh series re-anchor only until its first return is learned. Whoever submits first therefore decides which closes make up the series' history. (a) Warm-up denial: right after SeriesCreated, anyone can submit the two latest closes. The series then has 1 observation, and every earlier close fails with SeriesAlreadyObserved. The keeper's 25-day backfill is dead, and the series needs about 19 more live days before any round can open. Every round funded in that time voids at lock. (b) Variance steering: with Hermes history, anyone can backfill a hand-picked, increasing subsequence of closes. Returns are scaled by r^2*DAY/gap, which is unbiased only when the days are not chosen with hindsight. After warm-up, the EWMA (lambda 0.94) only halves that error every ~11 days. Too small a variance gives narrow bands, cheap far bands and bigger LP losses, up to the deposit bound. The same skip is open to anyone whenever the keeper misses a day.

> **Verifier.** Holds as stated, with two limits on the impact. Warm-up denial delays a new series by about 19 days: rounds funded in that time void and depositors are refunded, so no principal is lost. Variance steering is practical mainly in the window between series_create and the keeper's backfill. After warm-up it needs the keeper to miss a day, and a single skipped close gives little leverage. The architecture.md line 150 and the series_observe docstring claim a missed day can be submitted later, and that is false once a later close has been observed. Fix: enforce the next index, allowing a skip only after a delay, and bound or authority-gate the cold-start anchor.

> **Verifier.** Confirmed. series_observe does not require the next day, and a series stops accepting earlier closes after its first learned return. Anyone who wins the race right after series_create can (a) wipe out the keeper's 25-close backfill, delaying the first opened round by about 19 closes (griefing only: rounds funded in that time void, depositors are paid first, no principal is lost), or (b) hand-pick the 20 warm-up closes from Pyth history to push the variance low or high. That skews the band step and the prior bell for weeks (half-life about 11 days), and the series is permanent (no reset; its PDA is keyed feed x quote mint). Since the keeper submits each close about 60 s after it, steering after warm-up needs the keeper to be down. LP loss stays within the LMSR bound (about the deposit). The skip-after-48h fix does not protect the backfill, because every historic close is already older than 48 h. A sounder fix is to let only the authority observe while the series is cold (or have series_create fix a consecutive start index), then require the next index, with a delay-gated skip (optionally also a cap on the gap) after warm-up. architecture.md:150 and the comment at series.rs:142-146 should be corrected.

**Proposed fix.** Store next_index on Series and require index == next index with a round. Allow a skip only once now > close_of(next) + a delay (e.g. 48 h), so a close Pyth was silent across can still be passed but a live day cannot be jumped. Restrict the fresh-series anchor too (e.g. index must be >= the creation day - WARMUP - 5, and the backfill must then run consecutively), or let the authority run the one-time backfill inside series_create's lifetime. Correct the architecture.md sentence.

### An LP claim for a pre-open deposit uses ~114K CU; the app's Collect buttons budget 80K for one item, so it fails

*program-security · integration · reviewer medium, verifiers medium, medium · hour*

For a tranche that joined before open, `tranche_terms` recomputes the whole 64-bin prior (32 exp_wad) plus `liquidity_for_deposit` and `tranche_pnl`. That is roughly 114K CU, against about 20K for a redeem. Ticket.tsx and Yours.tsx build the collect transaction with 60_000 + 20_000 * items. One tranche gets 80K, a tranche plus a position 100K, and 12 tranches 300K against about 1.37M needed. On a settled round those transactions fail every time with 'exceeded CUs meter', including for a round's creator, whose seed is always a pre-open tranche. LpPanel's single Claim uses the 120K default plus an idempotent create-ATA, and the keeper's grace-period claim uses 120K. Both have only ~5.5K headroom (not measured with the ATA instruction).

> **Verifier.** Holds as stated. The creator's pre-open claim_lp on a settled round used 114,330 CU (measured on LiteSVM, Token-2022, 8 decimals). Ticket.tsx:189 and Yours.tsx:138 (not :141) budget 60K + 20K per item, so their Collect button always fails when the chunk includes a pre-open tranche and there are not enough cheap redeems in the same chunk to cover it. Void rounds are unaffected. LpPanel's Claim (120K plus ensureAta) and the keeper's grace-period claim (120K, no ATA) are near the limit but not shown to fail. The funds are not stuck: they can be claimed through LpPanel or a transaction with a higher CU limit.

> **Verifier.** Confirmed as stated, with small corrections to the numbers. A settled claim_lp for a tranche deposited before open uses about 114.3K CU; one that joined after open uses about 24K, and a redeem about 13K. The Collect buttons in Ticket.tsx:189 and Yours.tsx:138 budget 60K + 20K per item, so any collect that includes a creator's or early LP's tranche fails every time. LpPanel's single Claim still works, with about 1.3K CU to spare, not 5.5K, because the idempotent create-ATA on an existing ATA costs about 4.4K. It fails only if the ATA has to be created. The keeper's claim at 120K fits with about 5.7K to spare. No funds are at risk, because the House-tab Claim and the keeper still work.

**Proposed fix.** Budget claim_lp at about 130K per item in the app and the keeper, and batch at most ~10 claims per transaction. Better, cache the prior: store the opening weights once (they are already in `weights` at open, so snapshot them to the tranche on first claim, or to a per-ladder field) so the claim does not recompute 32 exponentials. Add an SDK claimComputeUnits helper like tradeComputeUnits.

### After the 24h void grace, anyone can void a round that could still settle; a losing trader prefers the void

*program-security · security · reviewer low, verifiers low, low · hour*

ladder_settle has no deadline (only now >= settles_at), and ladder_void becomes callable at settles_at + 24h. Both are permissionless. The settlement update stays postable from Hermes history, so after 24 h the outcome goes to whoever lands first. Void refunds cost basis, so losing traders and losing LPs prefer it. docs/architecture.md says 'A void pays nothing, so a losing trader never prefers voiding to settling', which is false: the settler's bounty only makes settling attractive to third parties. The race exists only if nobody settled for 24 h (keeper outage, or a Hermes or RPC outage).

> **Verifier.** The claim holds as stated. Settlement has no deadline, and ladder_void is permissionless from settles_at + 24h while the round is still OPEN, so after a 24h settlement outage the first transaction to land decides between void and settle. A void refunds cost basis, so losers prefer it. The doc sentence in docs/architecture.md lines 99-100 ("a losing trader never prefers voiding to settling") is false: the void pays no bounty to the caller, but it does refund losers. This only happens after a full day with no settlement, so it is low severity: a liveness and doc problem, not a way to steal funds.

> **Verifier.** Once 24h pass after settles_at with no settlement, ladder_void and ladder_settle are both permissionless and have no ordering between them, so whichever lands first wins. Void refunds holders from the vault after deposits, so losing traders prefer it. That makes the docs/architecture.md line 'a losing trader never prefers voiding to settling' false: the thing a void pays nothing of is the settler's bounty. The race needs 24 hours of total settlement failure, even though winners and bounty hunters are paid to settle, and it moves expected value between holders without breaking solvency. Impact is low: fix the doc, and optionally add monitoring for unsettled rounds or let the void path settle when a valid price update is supplied.

**Proposed fix.** Correct the doc claim. Optionally lengthen VOID_GRACE_SECS, or give settlement priority, e.g. let settle run even on a voided round until every claim starts (complex). The practical fix is redundant settlers and alerting when a round passes settles_at + 1h unsettled.

### ladder_settle feeds the series a close with confidence up to step/2 (up to 10%), while series_observe requires 1%

*program-security · algorithm · reviewer low, verifiers low, info · hour*

series_observe checks the close with step_bps = 200, so conf <= 1%. settle_handler checks with the round's step_bps (20..2000), so conf <= step/2, which reaches 3-10% on volatile memecoin anchors. It then calls series.observe on that price unconditionally. The variance learner therefore takes prints through settlement that it would refuse through observe. That weakens the stated rule that the series learns only from the 1%-conf settlement instant.

> **Verifier.** ladder_settle folds its settlement print into the series variance after checking confidence only against the round's band (conf <= step/2, up to 10% of the price when step = 2000 bps). series_observe requires conf <= 1%. The learner therefore takes noisier closes through settlement than through observe. The price is still the unbiased Pyth aggregate at the close, and each print has only 6% EWMA weight, so the effect is a small, non-exploitable inconsistency and not a manipulation vector.

> **Verifier.** ladder_settle feeds the series a close at the round's own confidence bar (conf <= step/2, which is sigma/8 and ranges from 0.1% to 10%). series_observe uses a fixed 1% bar, so the learner accepts some wider-confidence closes through settlement than through observe, and some tighter ones are refused only by observe. Because the bar scales with sigma, the added variance noise is at most about 1.6% relative, biased toward wider bands. No docs state a 1% learning rule, and nobody loses funds. This is a consistency nit (info), not a soundness flaw.

**Proposed fix.** In settle_handler, call series.observe only when conf*20000 <= price*200 (the same bar as series_observe); otherwise leave the day for series_observe or skip it.

### Whoever submits closes chooses which ones the series learns from; worst at warm-up

*algorithm · algorithm · reviewer high, verifiers medium, medium · day*

series_observe accepts any close later than last_at (and, while observations==0, any close at all), and a skipped close can never be submitted afterwards. So whoever submits first chooses which closes enter the variance. Against a new series this means choosing all 20 warm-up closes. The keeper's backfill of 10 closes per learn pass, every 4th pass, leaves gaps an attacker can jump into, after which the keeper's remaining submissions fail with SeriesAlreadyObserved. The attacker cannot choose the prices, but can pick calm pairs of closes and spans over gaps. The chosen sigma then sets the band width and prior of every round until EWMA decay washes it out (half-life of about 11 closes).

> **Verifier.** During a new series' warm-up (and in a keeper outage), anyone can pick which past closes the series learns from. It works because series_observe accepts any later close, allows any earlier re-anchor while observations == 0, and never lets a skipped close back in, while the keeper backfills only 10 closes per learn pass and gives up on SeriesAlreadyObserved. Under the steady-state keeper cadence no close can be skipped. The size of the effect (the sigma ratios and house loss) is the reviewer's estimate and was not reproduced here.

> **Verifier.** The mechanism and numbers hold: whoever submits a new series' warm-up closes first can pick a favourable subsequence. While the series is cold, the pool includes any verifiable historical close, not just the keeper's 25-close backfill. The keeper then gives up on the closes it lost to the attacker. The bias is permanent until EWMA decay, because no admin reset exists. Exposure is limited to the warm-up window of each newly created series, which only the authority creates. Operators can largely close that window off-chain by landing the backfill right behind series_create. After warm-up, only keeper outages allow skips, with small bias. The proposed skip-delay fix (a) does not cover backfill, because backfill closes are already old. The right fix: warm-up closes must be consecutive has_round indices from a genesis index recorded at series_create, and a gap resets observations to 0.

**Proposed fix.** Make skipping impossible, or at least costless to the estimate. Options: (a) require index == next_round_index(last) unless the caller proves the skipped close is unobservable (not feasible on chain), or allow a skip only after a delay (e.g. accept close N+k only once now > close(N+1) + a few hours, so an honest submitter has time to fill the gap); (b) during warm-up, require consecutive has_round indices (a gap resets the warm-up count); (c) have series_create take a genesis index and require warm-up closes to be exactly the 20 after it. At minimum, count skipped closes and refuse to warm up with more than a few.

### Void: a self-dealing pair takes up to about half of honest traders' refunds

*algorithm · economics · reviewer medium, verifiers low, low · day*

The deposit-first split protects the house, but the traders' pot is shared by cost basis (net_paid). Wallet A buys a bin, B pumps it, and A sells into B. B's basis then exceeds what the pair actually left in the pool by A's realised gain. In a void, B's share of the traders' pot is inflated by that gain, which is paid by the other traders still holding positions, not by the house. The architecture doc's 'the pair nets zero' holds only when the pair are the only open positions.

> **Verifier.** The mechanics are accurate: in a void, open positions split the traders' pot by remaining cost basis. A wash pair (A sells to B at a gain g) therefore takes C*g/(C+B) from the other open traders, not from depositors. This is intended and documented in the void_pots comment and in architecture.md ("those gains are paid by the traders who stayed, not the house"). The doc's claim is "nets zero from the house", which is accurate, not "nets zero" overall. Still open: a cheap option on a rare, oracle-driven void, paid by the other traders. It is worth one explicit sentence about self-dealing in the docs and does not need a code change.

> **Verifier.** In a void, holders who stayed pay for gains a self-dealing pair realised before it. The pair nets gA*cH/(cP+cH) from honest open basis cH, and zero from depositors. This is a known consequence of the depositors-first policy that docs/architecture.md chose on purpose: it says "nets zero from the house" and "shares its losses with the others who stayed", and the round-2 audit gave the same formula. The only effective program fix is a marked-value refund, min(net_paid, sell-back value on the frozen curve). A per-wallet cap on net contribution does nothing against a two-wallet pair. The practical fix is to name this case in the architecture doc and the ticket's void copy, and to make voids rarer.

**Proposed fix.** Refund open positions by their share of the net cash they actually contributed. Track per position the realised proceeds of sales (or basis released at the sale price rather than pro rata to cost), and cap each wallet's void claim at its net contribution. Alternatively split the traders' pot by min(net_paid, marked value at the last trade). At minimum, document that in a void the other traders who stayed bear a self-dealer's realised gains.

### Today's bands ignore yesterday's close, and whoever opens the round can choose

*algorithm · algorithm · reviewer low, verifiers low, low · hour*

opens_at = settles_at - 24h is exactly the previous day's close. The keeper's pass opens seeding rounds before it settles open ones, and learn() runs only every 4th pass, so a round usually opens on a variance that lacks yesterday's return. Because both open and observe are permissionless at that instant, whoever opens can choose to observe first or not, which moves sigma by sqrt(0.94 + 0.06·r²/v) on a big day.

> **Verifier.** A round funded more than 24h ahead opens at settles_at - 24h, which is normally the previous day's close. ladder_open reads series.var_wad and does not require that close to have been observed. series_observe and ladder_open are both permissionless, so whoever opens first decides whether yesterday's return goes into sigma. On a 3-sigma day that is a factor of about 1.22; the downward swing is at most 0.97x, never 0.82x. The keeper by default opens seeding rounds before it settles open ones, and learn() runs every 4th pass after pass(), so it usually opens on the stale variance. The loss figures from kl.py can't be checked, because the script is not in the repo. What the opener actually gains is the gap between two defensible sigma estimates, so the impact is low. On the US spring-forward day the round opens an hour before the previous close, so that close cannot be included at all.

> **Verifier.** The claim holds. A round funded ahead can be opened right at the previous day's close. ladder_open reads the series variance without checking whether that close has been observed, and the keeper opens seeding rounds before it settles or learns, so by default the round opens on variance that is one close stale. Because observe is permissionless, an opener can also choose either variance. On most days the effect is well under 0.4% of deposit and only approaches it after tail moves, and it is a smaller lever than the opener's existing choice of p0 within 60 s and of the open time, which sets the window. Do not add the proposed last_at require, because an unobservable close would make rounds unopenable and they would void. Instead, open on the settlement-rule update at opens_at, which is yesterday's close, and have open_handler observe it before band_width. That removes both the p0 choice and the sigma choice. Reordering the keeper only fixes the default.

**Proposed fix.** In ladder_open, require series.last_at >= settles_at - ROUND_SECS (the previous round-day's close has been learned), or let open observe that close itself when a price update is passed. Reorder the keeper to learn before opening.

### Calendar-time scaling underestimates volatility for weekday and equity series

*algorithm · algorithm · reviewer low, verifiers low, low · hour*

observe divides r² by the calendar seconds elapsed. On CLOCK_NEW_YORK_WEEKDAYS, the Friday-to-Monday return spans 3 days but carries about 1 trading day of variance. A 7-day clock on an anchor that moves only on weekdays adds two near-zero weekend returns every week.

> **Verifier.** Confirmed with a smaller effect. Series::observe scales r^2 by calendar seconds, so on CLOCK_NEW_YORK_WEEKDAYS the Friday-to-Monday return is divided by about 3. If weekend variance were zero, the learned sigma would be about 0.93x the trading-day sigma. Real Friday-to-Monday variance is about 1.1 to 1.3 trading days, and the xStock feeds print on weekends, so the actual bias is a bit smaller. The Monday round (and every round, through the EWMA) opens with slightly narrow bands. That costs LPs roughly 0.05 to 0.1% of the deposit per round. The 7-day (x0.83) case arises only if an equity anchor is put on CLOCK_NEW_YORK, which the docs rule out. Holidays on the weekday clock add near-zero returns too. Fix: scale by the count of has_round indices between closes. Audit round 3 proposed this and it was never implemented.

> **Verifier.** On CLOCK_NEW_YORK_WEEKDAYS series, observe divides the Friday-to-Monday squared return by about 3 days even though it carries about 1 trading day of variance. Learned sigma therefore comes out about 7% low. Replaying the repo backtest on real SPY and GLD data costs the house about 0.05 points (SPY) to 0.45 points (GLD) of deposit per round, against a baseline loss of 9 to 18%. The 7-day-clock case (x0.83) is hypothetical: no deployed or configured series pairs a weekday-only anchor with the 7-day clock. Fix: for weekday series, divide by the count of has_round days between the two closes rather than by elapsed seconds.

**Proposed fix.** For weekday series, scale by trading days (count the has_round indices between the two closes) rather than seconds. Keep equity anchors on the weekday clock.

### Whoever submits a cold series' first closes chooses them, and so sets its volatility and the LPs' loss

*series-oracle · economics · reviewer high, verifiers high, medium · day*

While observations < 20, series_observe accepts any close later than last_at, and any earlier close while observations == 0. There is no bound on the close's age or on the gap between closes, and the warm-up is a plain average of r^2*DAY/gap. So the first submitter can pick 21 increasing historical closes that the Pyth receiver will still accept (all of them back to the last guardian rotation, which on mainnet is a long history). By choosing pairs whose price barely moved they bias sigma low; by choosing big moves they bias it high. The biased variance sets the step and the prior bell of every round that opens while the EWMA recovers.

> **Verifier.** The claim holds as stated. series_observe does not bound how old a close may be or how many closes it may skip, and while observations == 0 it also accepts earlier closes. So whoever wins the race after series_create can choose which 20 or more historical closes form the plain-average warm-up (each one is the price the settlement rule fixes for that close, but the set is chosen). That sets a biased variance, which in turn sets the step and prior bell of every round opened while the EWMA recovers. Nothing can reset the variance and the series PDA cannot be recreated, so the bias persists until about 26 or more honest closes dilute it, or until a program upgrade. After warm-up the same freedom to skip exists but counts only 6% per observation. The quantitative figures are the reviewer's and were not re-run here.

> **Verifier.** While a series has fewer than 20 observations, series_observe checks only the per-close settlement rule and `at > last_at`. It never requires consecutive closes and never bounds the anchor's age, so whoever beats the keeper after series_create picks which verifiable historical closes form the warm-up average. That biases sigma, which sets the step and the prior bell of rounds opened over roughly the next 26 closes, and LPs who funded early carry the mispricing. The same skipping is possible after warm-up but only matters during keeper downtime, since each observation then weighs 6%. The window is one-time per series. It needs the attacker to front-run the keeper and then trade, and it is barely exploitable on devnet today, where only about 20 closes lie after the 4 Sep 2026 guardian rotation. It is a real flaw for mainnet or for later series. Fix: store a start or anchor bound at create time, and require the exact next has_round close (skips by the authority only), at least during warm-up.

**Proposed fix.** Bound the warm-up. (1) Have series_create fix the anchor index, or require the anchor to be at most about 45 days before now. (2) While observations < WARMUP, require each close to be the next has_round index after last_at, allowing at most one skipped close so a silent feed cannot block warm-up. (3) Optionally, after warm-up, refuse closes more than a few days old unless the close before them is also unobservable. Alternatively, let only the authority submit the 20 warm-up closes, since it already creates the series, and state that trust assumption in the docs.

### One forward-jump observation ends a new series' backfill; its funded rounds void for weeks

*series-oracle · integration · reviewer medium, verifiers low, low · day*

Once a series has one observation, only later closes are accepted. So while the keeper is backfilling (10 closes per learn pass, every 4th pass), anyone can submit the latest close. Every earlier close is then refused with SeriesAlreadyObserved, and the keeper marks them unobservable for good. Warm-up then needs about 20 live closes (about 27 calendar days on the weekday clock). Every round funded for those days cannot open: it voids at its lock, and on the STOOK-style Token-2022 mints depositors lose the transfer fee in and out. The architecture doc says a missed day can always be submitted later. That is only true until someone submits a later day.

> **Verifier.** While a new series is backfilling (observations between 1 and 19), anyone can submit a later close. series_observe then refuses every earlier close with SeriesAlreadyObserved, and the keeper drops those closes for good. Warm-up then waits for the remaining (20 - observations) live closes, and any round funded for those days never opens and voids. On 1%-fee Token-2022 mints, depositors lose the transfer fee in and out.

The window is about a minute after series_create: the keeper's learn runs every ~20s, 10 closes per pass, over a 25-close backfill. Closing it takes a deliberate griefer who watches for new series. The worst case (about 19 closes, about 27 calendar days) needs the griefer to front-run the keeper's second observation. If the jump lands after the first learn pass, the delay is about 11 weekday closes.

docs/architecture.md:148-150 and the series_observe_handler comment are inaccurate: a close can be backfilled only until a later close has been observed.

> **Verifier.** While a new series is backfilling, a stranger who submits a later close once observations >= 1 cuts off the remaining earlier closes, and warm-up then needs 20 minus the observations so far in live closes. ## Verdict per lens

**program-security.** PROGRAM SECURITY lens, at HEAD 9672fff. Baseline: `cargo test -p sooth_core --lib` gives 111 passed, and the SDK vitest suite passes. I added one scratch file, packages/sdk-solana/tests/zz_scratch.test.ts, ran it, then deleted it. The tree still has lib.rs modified, src/zz_scratch.rs and tests/zz_scratch_series_liveness.test.ts. Those belong to a concurrent agent and I did not touch them.

I found no way to steal funds. Every account-substitution, signer and redirect path I checked holds:

- **Series substitution:** `ladder_create` binds `series.quote_mint` to the mint, and the ladder PDA to (series, index). `open` and `settle` pin `address = ladder.series`. `observe` only ever uses the series' own feed_id. A fake Series fails Anchor's owner and discriminator checks, and `series_create` and `series_set` are authority-only, so the series cannot be front-run.
- **Positions and tranches:** each is bound to its ladder and owner. Redeem and claim close into `owner`.
- **Payouts after the 30-day grace:** a stranger cannot redirect a payout. LiteSVM: before the grace ends a stranger gets `LadderNotYours`. After it, passing the stranger's own token account fails on `token::authority = owner`, and passing the owner's account pays the owner.
- **Re-creating a day:** not possible. Close needs now >= settles_at, and create needs now + 15 min <= settles_at. A weekend index on a weekday series is refused by `has_round`, and an out-of-range index by the 31-day window.
- **Token-2022 harvest in close:** the hand-built CPI is correct. The data [26,4] is `TransferFeeExtension` then `HarvestWithheldTokensToMint`, the mint and vault are writable, and the existing $STOOK LiteSVM test closes a vault that holds withheld fees.
- **Oracle reads:** `read_price_update` checks that the Pyth receiver owns the account. The settlement rule prev_publish < t <= publish (gap <= 30 s) admits exactly one update. Open checks age (60 s), clock skew, feed, signature floor and a 1% conf ceiling.
- **Integer overflow:** checked in `band_width`, `prior`, `observe`, `tranche_terms` and `fee_bps_at`, and none is reachable:
  - variance <= 9e16 and the window <= 24 h;
  - WAD squared fits in i128;
  - the observe divisor is > 0 on that path;
  - `tranche_terms` works on a deposit that is <= the deposit_total `open` already sized.
- **Heap:** the frame is prepended by `withHeap` on every SDK, app and keeper path.

Measured compute (LiteSVM, Token-2022): create 48K, open 95K (keeper allows 200K), a 64-bin reach-8 trade on a grown curve 140K (the SDK requests 221K), settle 23K. An LP claim for a pre-open deposit is about 114K, because it recomputes the opening prior. The app's Collect buttons budget far less than that (finding 2).

The real problems are these:
1. **Anyone can choose which daily closes a series learns from.** A cheap attack can delay a new series' 20-close warm-up by weeks, or skew its volatility during the backfill.
2. **Collecting a pre-open LP deposit fails from two of the app's buttons,** which don't request enough compute.
3. **After the 24 h void grace, a round that could still settle can be voided.** This contradicts the documented claim that a losing trader never prefers a void.
4. **A settlement can feed the series a price with confidence up to step/2** (up to 10%), while `series_observe` requires 1%.
5. **Some code comments and docs are out of date.**

**algorithm.** The market maths holds up. Solvency, tranche accounting, settle/claim/redeem exactness, the fee ramp, band_width/prior SDK parity and tranche_terms were all confirmed by tests I wrote and ran. In each case every result matched: zero mismatches, zero breaches. Two economic weaknesses are real. First, whoever submits closes to series_observe chooses which closes the series learns from, so a series' volatility can be steered. This is severe when a series is new: picking closes from 40 to 60 days of history leaves the learned sigma at 0.32 to 0.41x the honest value, which costs the house about 10 to 16% of its deposit per round to a trader who knows the true spread. It is mild after a keeper outage. Second, in a void, a self-dealing pair of wallets can take up to about half of the honest traders' refund. The house's deposit stays whole in both.

Checks that passed (scratch tests in the repo and in /private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-mohammadzakerirad-Sooth-soo--agents-analyze/3ab9aa51-1869-4558-827a-adf4e83ef8f5/scratchpad; the repo was returned to git-clean):
(1) Solvency: 300 fuzzed rounds, each with 1 to 5 deposits before open, open windows from 900 s to 86,400 s, sigma from 0.3 to 8.3%, bands and tents of any shape including edge tents, joins after open and partial sells. A further 60,000 random trades were run too. The cash >= max_payout check never refused a trade. The smallest margin was 0.025% of deposits, which is the 0.9999 haircut. For every bin, the sum of redemptions equals payout[k] exactly. The sum of tranche principals was always at or below lp_pool (worst case -7 base units), and tranche fees at or below fees_lp (worst case -1).
(2) tranche_terms: the pool's b minus the sum of the recomputed early-deposit b's is 0 to 3 wei. A deposit made before open and a join made right after open get bit-identical b, join odds, P&L and fees. This also holds for a round opened 2 h late (step clamped to 20), at MIN_STEP with MIN_VAR (var_bands_e9 250,000,000) and at MAX_STEP.
(4) The fee ramp: 200 bps at 6 h, 201 at 6 h minus 60 s, 350 at 3.5 h, 499 at 1 h plus 1 s, then 500 from 1 h on. It is monotonic over 30,000 seconds and the SDK's feeBpsAt matches at every boundary. Nobody can profit from the timing: both legs of a trade pay a fee, and an LP's rebate is 0.9 x its share of b, which is below 1.
(6) SDK parity: openingTerms matched band_width, var_bands_e9, the prior's sum and w[31] on 400 random (variance, window) pairs with 0 mismatches.
(3) The void split never shorts depositors, and claims come out the same in any order.

**series-oracle.** The calendar and settlement machinery holds up. close_of is correct for NY and NY-weekday clocks through DST: switch days are right because of the 3 AM floor, weekends have no round, and index math cannot overflow for any reachable index. round_times gives a canonical window for rounds funded weeks ahead (opens at close minus 24h, locks 1h before the close). A round created late gets a shorter window, and its lock gap is still clamped to at least 2 minutes. MAX_LEAD (31 days) and MIN_ROUND (15 minutes) are enforced at create. The earlier void-then-recreate hole is closed, because ladder_close needs now >= settles_at and create needs now + 900 <= settles_at. The settlement rule's edge cases behave as follows. An update published exactly at T with prev < T passes. An update with prev == T is refused. A gap of exactly 30 s passes and 31 s fails. conf exactly equal to half a band passes, since the comparison is lhs <= rhs. With MIN_STEP at 20 bps the conf ceiling is 10 bps, which the measured weekday SPYX close (2.8 bps) and the crypto stand-ins clear. A settle never reverts because observe fails. After warm-up there is no real skipper or front-runner gain: a close's price is unique, settle and the keeper observe every day, and a series stays warmed once warmed, so a Wormhole guardian rotation on mainnet never halts a warmed series. It only costs closes the keeper missed for more than 24h across the rotation, and it means a new series has to warm from about 21 live closes. The weak spot is the cold start. While a series has fewer than 20 observations, whoever submits first chooses which historical closes teach it, with no age or gap bound. A LiteSVM run on the HEAD .so shows this biases the learned sigma about 3.6x low and makes the first weeks of rounds cost LPs about 23% of their deposit to anyone who knows the real sigma. The same freedom lets one forward jump end the keeper's backfill, so warm-up can be delayed by up to about 20 live closes. Rounds funded in that time void, and the keeper spends Pyth posts on each of them all day. Separately, docs overstate that missed days can always be backfilled, and a few code comments are stale. I could not verify the claimed devnet guardian rotation of 2026-09-04 without RPC access. My scratch test was deleted. The tree still shows a lib.rs change and src/zz_scratch.rs that belong to parallel auditors; I did not touch them.

**keeper-infra.** The keeper, tape and infrastructure are sound on the points this lens set out to check. No secret is logged: Hermes errors print only the path and status (index.mjs:61), PYTH_API_KEY goes only in a request header, and deploy.sh leaves out .env*; the only ignored local secret file is apps/stook/.env.local. In learn(), the `break` only stops the current series, so one series cannot starve the others. Blockhash retries stop after 5 attempts. Skipping an unobservable close is safe because series_observe accepts any later close (series.rs:153). Sweeps are limited to zero-owed positions, and the program enforces this too (ladder.rs:1353). Grace payouts can only reach the owner (may_collect plus the owner constraints). Fees are collected before the close. Token-2022 ATAs are derived with mint.owner. Every getProgramAccounts offset matches the Rust layout: I computed Ladder.status at 8+1912, LADDER_SIZE is 1936, a Position's ladder is at offset 8, a Tranche's ladder at 16, and SERIES_SIZE equals Series::SPACE. keepalive.sh's absolute-path pgrep is correct, and the tape's Raydium CLMM and Orca Whirlpool field offsets are correct.
