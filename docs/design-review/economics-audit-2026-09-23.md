# Economics audit — 2026-09-23

Twenty-one blind agents over the shipped `sooth_core.so`: four lenses (solvency, payout, house, design-vs-claims), each finding then attacked by two skeptics (one engineer, one economist). Nothing was refuted. Scratch tests were run against the binary and deleted; the repo carries the fixes and their tests.

## The answer, in plain terms

**Where the money comes from.** The pool's cash is every LP deposit plus every payment traders have made, minus what sellers have been paid out. A winner is paid `shares × level(winning bin)` from that cash. After every single trade the program checks `cash ≥ max payout over all 64 bins` (instructions/ladder.rs:545) and refuses the trade otherwise; it checks again at settlement. So a trader can be owed far more than the LP put in, but only because that trader (and the losers) put the rest in themselves. Verified example: LP deposits 1,000; a trader pumps one bin as hard as possible and ends up owed 4,750, having paid 3,787. The pool holds 4,787 and can pay. The LP's loss is capped by the LMSR bound at `b·ln64 = 0.9999 × deposit`; nobody can lose more than they put in, and the pool can never owe more than it holds. This held for ~28,000 randomized trades, ~1,700 late LP joins, at 6/9/18 decimals, all 64 outcomes.

**What 15× and 4x/3x/2x/1x mean.** They are not returns. 4/3/2/1 are payout levels per share by distance from the centre of a line. Every share is priced at the probability-weighted sum of those levels, so you buy expected value. Worked number: on a fresh 64-bin ladder each bin is 1/64. A reach-4 line pays 4+3+2+1+... across 7 bins, summing to 16 levels, so one share costs 16/64 = 0.25 and pays 4 on an exact hit: 16×. The founder's screen showed 10 shares for 2.66 paying 40: that is the same 0.25/share plus fee, and it is 1.6% odds priced fairly. The multiple collapses as you buy: 15.7× at 10 shares, 14.3× at 100, 5.0× at 1,000, 1.25× at the weight cap. Anyone can pick any range because everyone pays the current price for it. Program and SDK agree on these numbers to the base unit.

**Verdict.** The core design is correct: sizing, solvency, LP P&L telescoping, fee attribution and the SDK quote all match the spec and were confirmed on the shipped binary (92 Rust + 30 vitest tests). The double-buy showing only "Sell", and buy drawing left-to-right vs sell right-to-left, are frontend presentation (Ticket.tsx default side, Chart.tsx drag), not program behaviour; on-chain a second buy tops up the same position.

Must fix before launch:
1. **`collect_fees` has no status gate.** Anyone can drain creator/protocol fees while the market is Open, zeroing the settler's bounty and leaving LPs short on a void. One-line fix: require STATUS_SETTLED.
2. **Void path shorts LPs.** A void refunds traders at cost but anyone who sold at a gain keeps it, so LPs eat every realised gain (proven: LP gets 912 of 1,000; multi-bin pump-and-dump covers only 57% of basis, LPs get zero; four wallets under one owner net exactly the LP deposit). Only reachable if no settlement lands in 24h, but that is foreseeable and turns a void into a riskless LP drain. Decide the void policy (e.g. refund traders net of realised gains, or pro-rate LPs first) and fix the false "impossible" comments.

Should fix: the ticket hides a 0.5% slippage slack it signs; multiples and Collect totals ignore the 1% transfer fee (~2% overstated). Low: rounding dust of a few base units favours the trader at ≥9 decimals or very deep pools; the last LP claimant absorbs tranche rounding dust via the `.min(lp_pool)` clamp. Neither threatens solvency.

## Verdict per lens

**solvency** — SOLVENT. The pool can never owe a winner more than it holds: after ~28,000 randomized buys/sells of arbitrary shapes (bands, tents, plateaus, shapes hanging off both ladder edges, dust to max-exponent sizes) plus ~1,700 late LP joins at 6, 9 and 18 decimals, `cash >= payout[k]` held for every bin after every step, and the settle/claim path (winners + every tranche's rounded principal) fit inside cash for all 64 possible outcomes in all 120 runs. Maximum max_payout/cash observed: 0.999979 (directed: one bin driven to the W_MAX weight cap, where the margin collapses to exactly the 0.0001·D haircut plus the +1 base-unit roundings). What enforces it is the LMSR bound: with a single tranche cash − q_k = D − b·ln64 + b·ln(S/w_k) ≥ 0.0001·D; with late tranches the per-trade sums telescope per tranche to Σ_j [D_j − b_j·ln(S_j/w_k^j) + b_j·ln(S_f/w_k^f)] ≥ 0.0001·ΣD_j because liquidity_for_deposit sizes each b_j against the cheapest bin at its own join. The per-trade LadderInsolvent require never fired at textbook sizing (nor with the 0.9999 haircut removed) and fires in 10/10 seeds at 2× depth, so the sizing alone is sufficient and the require is a belt that also protects liveness-vs-bugs; the require alone would keep the vault solvent but would freeze a mis-sized market. The W_MAX cap bounds max_payout at ≈4.98·ΣD, but a trader must pay ≥ q_k − 0.9999·D to reach payout q_k, so the most any trader can net is 0.9999·ΣD (measured 0.9999× in the directed pump) — a "5x" payout figure is ~80% the trader's own money; a height-h line costs Σ mᵢ·pᵢ per share (up to h× a band), so the 4x/3x/2x/1x multiplier is priced, not free. Two real but bounded gaps: (1) integer rounding is not strictly against the trader at ≥9 decimals or on very large 6-decimal pools — exp_wad is quantized at 1e-18 absolute, so a large-|x| sell overpays the seller by ~1e-11·B, letting a buy-then-sell round trip net +137 base units (1.4e-7 tokens) at 9 decimals and +2 base units on a 10M-token 6-decimal pool; the 1% fee dwarfs it and the 0.0001·D haircut absorbs ~10^7 such trades; (2) a VOID after sequential pump-and-dumps on two different bins leaves the vault covering only 57% of basis_total — the code pro-rates refunds so nothing is over-drawn, but the "should be impossible" comment and "a void refunds what people paid" are false in that state and LPs then get zero. No finding lets the vault pay out more than it holds.

**payout** — The payout logic is correct and solvent. A trader is paid `shares × level(settled_bin)` from the market vault (`ladder_redeem`), and that money is always there because the program enforces `cash >= max(payout[])` after every trade (`instructions/ladder.rs:545`) and again at settlement (`:808`), where `cash = all deposits + all buy payments − all sell proceeds` (fees are kept outside cash). The LMSR bound guarantees the check never fails: the LP's worst-case loss is b·ln64 = 0.9999·deposit, so the winning band's payout is funded by the LP's bounded loss plus the losing traders' payments. The "15×" on the founder's screen is not free money: it is 40 payout / 2.66 paid for 10 shares of a reach-4 line, which is exactly the ~1.6%-odds price of the centre band (marginal cost per share = Σ levelᵢ·pᵢ = 16/64 = 0.25, confirmed bit-exact between program and SDK), and the multiplier collapses as size grows (15.7× at 10 shares → 14.3× at 100 → 5.0× at 1,000 on a 5,000-deposit round, and 1.25× when a bin is piled to the weight cap). "Anyone can pick any range and get 4x/3x/2x/1x" is correct by design: those are payout levels per share by distance from the centre, and every share costs its probability-weighted sum of those levels, so a trader buys expected value, not a guaranteed multiple. Simulated with a scratch Rust test (6 scenarios, 1,062 random trades + a pile-on to the cap; deleted afterwards) and cross-checked against the LiteSVM e2e on the real binary. Two UI concerns the founder raised (double-buy shows only Sell; buy draws left-to-right, sell right-to-left) are frontend issues outside this lens; on-chain, a second buy of the same (lo,hi,h) is a top-up of one position PDA, not a duplicate.

**lp** — THE HOUSE lens: the LP accounting is correct end to end on the settle path. (a) b is sized so a tranche's worst-case loss is exactly 0.9999·deposit, verified at the weight cap; (b) tranche_pnl telescopes to the pool's trading result for every bin, including a mid-market join, to 2e-14 tokens; (c) a late tranche earns fees only on volume after it joined, to 1 base unit; (d) the lp_pool / fees_lp caps never bind in any claim order — integer rounding (ceil+1 on buys, floor−1 on sells, floor gains / ceil losses) guarantees Σ principal ≤ lp_pool with ~1.5 base units of dust per trade left in the vault, never a shortfall. The expected-P&L claim −b·KL(final‖join) holds numerically per tranche. ONE REAL FINDING: (e) the void path does NOT return every LP its deposit. It does so only when no position was ever sold at a gain. void pot = deposit_total + Σ_sells(basis released − sale proceeds): a trader who bought cheap and sold into a pump keeps the gain, the pumper is refunded at cost, and the LPs eat the difference. Reproduced on the real binary (LiteSVM): LP seeded 1000, A buys 100 of bin 40 for 1.949, B buys 1500 for 623.09, A sells for 89.96, void → B refunded in full, A nets +88.01, LP gets 911.99 (short 88.01). The existing void e2e test passes only because its trader never sells. Not exploitable without a void, but a void that can be foreseen (feed silent in the 30 s window after settles_at, keeper down 24 h) turns this into a riskless LP drain. LP P&L sign: under uninformed (round-trip) flow trading P&L ≈ 0 and LPs keep fees (+4.39 on 1000 over 40 trades); under informed flow LPs lose up to 99.99% of deposit (1600 shares of the winning bin bought for 625 pays 1600; LP left with 18.85 + 4.95 fees of 1000). Payouts larger than the deposit are funded by traders' own payments (solvency check cash ≥ max_payout: 1000 LP, trader paid 3787.6 to be owed 4750; LP's exposure is capped at b·ln64 = 999.9). The 4x/3x/2x/1x on a line are payout LEVELS, not returns: a fresh h=4 tent costs 0.25/share and returns 16x on an exact hit, a 1-bin band costs 0.0156 and returns 64x, an 8-high tent costs 1.0/share and returns 8x.

**design** — The program does what the founder thinks in the ways that matter: every trade is gated by `cash >= max_payout` (instructions/ladder.rs:545), a deposit buys only the depth its own money covers (`b = 0.9999·D/ln(1/p_min)`, math/ladder.rs:292-310), so the pool can never owe more than it holds and no LP can lose more than they put in. The SDK quote is the number the program charges to the base unit (ladder-e2e.test.ts:99-111 sends every trade with limit == quote and compares balances and all 64 weights; ladder-stook.test.ts:105-120 does the same through a real 1% transfer-fee mint; ladder-token2022.test.ts at 8 dp). 92 Rust and 30 vitest tests pass on the shipped binary. The founder's "100x" worry is a display artefact of small stakes at long odds, not a hole: simulated LP 1000 / trader buying one band as hard as possible ends with the trader holding 4,000 shares for 3,030 paid, cash 4,000.10 vs payout 4,000, blended 1.32x. Of the UI claims, most are true; four are imprecise or false and one program gap undermines two of them: (1) `collect_fees` has no status gate, so a pre-emptive collect zeroes the settler's bounty and leaves LPs short in a void (proven on LiteSVM); (2) "You pay/You receive" hides a 0.5% slippage slack; (3) the x-your-stake column, "best case" and the Collect total ignore the transfer fee on the payout leg; (4) "reach changes where a dollar wins, not how much" is false: reach lowers the on-band multiple roughly as 1/h and off-centre rungs can pay below 1x. The double-buy / Sell-only / left-vs-right complaints are UI presentation, not program behaviour, and the working tree already carries uncommitted edits from another session addressing them.

## Findings that survived verification

### [low] (d) Rounding is not strictly against the trader: exp_wad's 1e-18 quantisation lets a large-|x| sell overpay the seller by ~1e-11·B, so a round trip can net a few base units at ≥9 decimals

exp_wad(x) for x ≈ −20 returns a value ≈ 2e-9·WAD with 1e-18 absolute error (4.8e-10 relative), floored. Multiplying a large weight by it leaves the new weight low by up to ~1e-9·WAD, so S' is low and sell proceeds b·ln(S/S') are high by ≈ b·1e-9/S ≈ 1e-11·B. The +1 base unit does not cover this once B·1e-11 exceeds one base unit (≈ B > 1e5 tokens at 6 dec, any realistic B at 9+ dec). The documented 'a round trip never pays' discipline (instructions/ladder.rs:500-504, math/ladder.rs a_round_trip_never_pays) therefore holds only at small pools or 6 decimals. Not exploitable: the 1% fee on the trade is ~10^8× the gain, and the 0.0001·D haircut absorbs ~10^7 such trades before the per-trade require would even fire. Solvency is unaffected.

> *Verifier:* Rounding is not strictly against the trader for large-|x| sells: the round trip buy/sell band(k,k) at |x|=20 on a bin pre-lifted to ~2 WAD nets the trader ≈1.5e-11·B, which exceeds the +1/−1 base-unit discipline once B ≳ 1e5 tokens at 6 decimals (measured with the handler's exact rounding: dec 6 D=1e7: +40 bu; dec 9 D=1e5: +347 bu; dec 9 D=1e6: +3,387 bu; dec 12 D=1e6: +3.37M bu; dec 18 D=10: +6,343 bu at |x|=11 since |x|=20 overflows u64 there). Two floor operations of equal magnitude are responsible, not one: exp_wad(x<0) flooring at 1e-18 (4.8e-10 relative at x=−20, multiplied by w≈1e9·WAD) AND wad_div(after, before) flooring the ratio (≈6.7e-8, so 1 unit ≈1.5e-11 relative) before ln_wad. Fixing either alone leaves ~7% of the gain; ceiling both (exp for x<0, ratio on sells) restores −2..−3 base units per round trip at 6/9/12 decimals. Not exploitable: the 1% fee is ~1e10× the gain and the 1e-4·D haircut absorbs ~3e7 such round trips before the cash ≥ max_payout require would fire; solvency is unaffected.

> *Verifier:* exp_wad truncates for x<0 (sum >> k), so a buy-then-sell of the same band at large |x| leaves the bin at w0·(1−δ) with δ up to ~5e-10 (exp_wad(−20)=2061153622 vs true 2061153622.44), and the sell overpays by ≈b·w0·δ/S. Because W_MAX caps w0·e^x and S≥64·WAD, the gain is bounded at ≈2e-11·b per round trip whatever |x| is; the +1/−1 base-unit discipline stops covering it once b ≳ 1.5e5 tokens at 6 decimals (measured: −2 bu at D=1e5 USDC, +45 bu = 4.5e-5 USDC at D=1e7 USDC) or at ≥9 decimals for ordinary pools (+476 bu at D=1e5 tok, 9 dec). So the instructions/ladder.rs:500-504 comment "never a positive one" is inaccurate at scale; the math-layer test already tolerates 1e-12 WAD of trader gain. Not exploitable and not a solvency issue: the extracting trade needs |x|=20, i.e. a buy leg of ≈16.5·b (≈4× the pool's deposit) with 1% fee on both legs ≈0.33·b — fee/gain ≈ 1.7e10 (not ~1e8) — and ~2e7 such trips to exhaust the 1e-4·D haircut; cash≥max_payout holds throughout. Fix is optional (ceil exp_wad for x<0, or reword the comment).

### [low] Sum of individually-rounded tranche principals can exceed lp_pool by dust; the .min(l.lp_pool) clamp is load-bearing for the last claimant

tranche_pnl reads ln of stored weights, which carry the exp/x-truncation drift, while lp_pool is integer cash − integer payout. Σ tranche_principal exceeded lp_pool in 40/2,560 (run,bin) settlements at 9 decimals (worst 2,216 base units = 2.2e-6 tokens = 1e-11 of lp_pool) and 2,211/2,560 at 18 decimals (worst 5.6e-11 tokens); never at 6 decimals. The clamp in claim_lp_handler (instructions/ladder.rs:1100-1102) makes the last LP to claim absorb it; the vault is never over-drawn. The doc claim 'rounding dust stays in the vault' is true at 6 decimals and slightly wrong in direction at 9+.

> *Verifier:* Sum of individually-rounded tranche principals can exceed lp_pool by dust; the .min(l.lp_pool) clamp at instructions/ladder.rs:1102 is load-bearing for the last LP claimant (without it, `l.lp_pool -= principal` would panic under overflow-checks and the last claim would revert). Cause: lp_pool = integer cash - exact integer payout[k], while tranche_pnl reads ln of the stored weights, which carry exp_wad Taylor truncation (~1.7e-16 relative per multiply, x level m <= 8) and wad_mul/wad_div truncation; the gap sum_t[delta_t*m_t(k) - B_t*ln(w'_t/w_t)] has either sign and scales with pool depth B x trade count x tent height, not with decimals per se. It becomes a visible overshoot when it exceeds the ~1-base-unit-per-trade margin (ceil+1 / floor-1) plus the per-tranche rounding-against. Reproduced: 0 hits at 6 decimals up to B ~ 2.4e8 tokens over 2,000 trades (raw drift still reached +571 units, absorbed by margin, so 6-decimal immunity is parametric, not structural); at 9 decimals 0 hits up to ~1e7-token pools but 95/2,560 hits with B ~ 1e9 tokens, worst +156,493 base units (1.6e-4 tokens, ~1e-14 of lp_pool); at 18 decimals nearly every settlement (2,539-3,803 of 2,560-3,840), worst 2.3e-14 tokens. The vault is never over-drawn (vault = cash + lp_pool + fees_lp + fees_creator + fees_protocol, all draws clamped); the last LP absorbs the dust. docs/architecture.md:210 ('rounding dust stays in the vault') holds at 6 decimals for realistic pools and is wrong in direction at 9+ decimals or very deep pools.

> *Verifier:* Σ tranche_principal can exceed lp_pool by dust and the .min(l.lp_pool) clamp in claim_lp_handler (instructions/ladder.rs:1100-1102) is what keeps the last claimant from over-drawing; the vault is never short. The cause is not principal rounding (tranche_principal rounds against the tranche) nor generic per-trade truncation drift (that is ~1e-16·B and is always covered by the ceil+1/floor−1 base unit per trade at ≤9 decimals). It is exp_wad at large negative exponents: a sell with |delta/b|·h near MAX_TRADE_EXPONENT returns exp(−19)·1e18 ≈ 5.6e9 WAD-units, whose one-unit truncation is ~2e-10 relative, so the stored weight (and tranche_pnl) understates the liability on the sold bin by up to ~2e-10·B tokens per such sell, while payout[k] is exact. The overshoot appears only if that bin settles; for every other bin the same drift leaves extra dust in the vault, so the doc's "rounding dust stays in the vault" is right in the normal regime and wrong in direction only for a max-size sell on the settled bin at 9+ decimals (at 6 decimals it needs B ≳ 1e4·n_trades tokens). Materiality: ≤ ~2e-10·B per event (≈$2.5e-5 on B=$250k), roughly seven orders of magnitude below the LP fee the same sell pays into fees_lp. Severity: informational / doc footnote.

### [medium] Void refunds can be short: after sequential pump-and-dumps on two different bins the vault covers 57% of basis_total (pro-rated by design, LPs get zero)

cash − basis_total = ΣD − Σ(realized trader gains). One bin's realized gains are bounded by b·ln64 ≤ 0.9999·D (LMSR), but once one bin holds most of the mass every other bin is nearly free, so a second pump-and-dump on another bin realizes another ≈2·D against a new top-buyer. The trade invariant cash ≥ max_payout stays true (only one bin can win), but the void obligation Σ net_paid adds across bins. redeem's pro-rata (redemption(): net_paid × min(1, vault/basis)) prevents any over-draw, and lp_share gives LPs max(0, vault − basis) = 0. So: not a vault-solvency breach, but instructions/ladder.rs:895-896 ('a shortfall — which the accounting should make impossible') and :872-873 / architecture 'a void refunds what people PAID' are false in reachable states, and the void path silently shifts loss from realized-gain traders to top-buyers and LPs. Needs an oracle failure (no settlement within VOID_GRACE) to matter.

> *Verifier:* Void refunds can be short and the shortfall is unbounded across bins: after sequential pump-and-dumps on two bins the vault (cash + fees) covers 57.17% of basis_total; on 3 bins 41%, on 7 bins 21%. Reproduced to the base unit against trade_handler's exact accounting: D = 1,000 tokens (6 dec), n = 10·b shares per leg; final vault 2,679,869,596 vs basis_total 4,687,247,110; cash ≥ max_payout held at every trade (tightest margin 100,034 units). Identity: vault − basis_total = ΣD − Σ(sale proceeds − basis released) holds exactly (−2,007,377,514). redeem's pro-rata (net_paid × min(1, void_vault/void_basis)) prevents over-draw and lp_share returns 0 to LPs. The comments at instructions/ladder.rs:872-873 ("a void refunds what people PAID") and :895-896 ("a shortfall — which the accounting should make impossible") are false in reachable states; docs/architecture.md makes no such statement, but docs/design-review/README.md's "LPs receive the residual, which equals F plus fees exactly" is the same false belief. If one party owns all four wallets, a void nets it exactly D (the entire LP deposit); the single-bin variant stays covered (1.016) but still nets a self-dealer 0.96·D. Reachable only through void_handler's never_settled branch (no settlement within VOID_GRACE_SECS = 24h).

> *Verifier:* Reachable and reproducible (cover 0.5717 with two pumped bins, 0.41 with three; single bin never below 1.0). But it is a wrong comment, not a solvency or extraction issue, and severity should be low: (a) basis is a sum over mutually exclusive positions and no LMSR property ever bounded it; cover < 1 means basis_total > vault ≥ max_payout, i.e. the remaining holders collectively paid more than any settlement could pay them (4.69B basis vs 2.40B max payout in the example), and the pro-rata void still pays them more in aggregate (2.68B) and, per head, no less in expectation (57% certain vs a ~50/50 lottery) than settlement would; (b) the void's real loser vs settlement is the LP (0 vs 167M–2.57B), which is the documented residual rule and already ≈fully realised in the single-bin wash (LP void residual 39.6M of 1,000M) — the second bin caps LP loss at zero rather than adding to it; (c) every void refund is ≤ net_paid and LP ≤ deposit, so nothing is extractable through the void, and the pro-rata rule is implemented and unit-tested ('a shortfall is shared, not raced for'); (d) only instructions/ladder.rs:872-873 and :895-896 (and the stale docs/design-review/README.md:133 'LPs receive the residual, which equals F plus fees exactly') are wrong — docs/architecture.md makes no such claim; (e) preconditions: a void plus ≥ ~2×D of near-certainty buying across ≥2 bins (|x| = 3 on two bins leaves cover 1.42). Fix the comments to state the pro-rata rule; the 'unwind value' mitigation would not increase what traders receive (total unwind value 1.57B < 2.68B already paid). If predictable voids on equity feeds are a concern, that is the separate, already-known create-time publishing-hours check.

### [low] Double-buy of the same range is a top-up of one position PDA on-chain (UI shows only Sell is a frontend issue)

The position account is seeded by (ladder, owner, lo, hi, h) with init_if_needed, so buying the same range twice adds shares and net_paid to one account; it is not a duplicate and is fully redeemable. The founder's 'shows sell button only after clicking a range I already bought' and the left-to-right/right-to-left drawing are UI-state issues outside this lens, not program bugs.

> *Verifier:* Buying the same shape twice from one wallet tops up a single LadderPosition PDA seeded by (ladder, owner, lo, hi, h): shares and net_paid are summed, sells release basis proportionally, and settled redemption pays shares × level(settled_bin) from that one account, which is then closed (verified on LiteSVM: 20+15 shares of band(30,34) → shares 35,000,000, net_paid = sum of both totals, redeemed 35,000,000 gross at bin 33). Caveat: 'same' means byte-identical args; the program does not canonicalise shapes, so an off-ladder lo/hi (validate allows −8..71) or an h larger than the shape's reach yields a payoff-identical shape on a separate PDA. Each such account remains independently correct and redeemable, and the UI (Chart.tsx clamps bins to 0..63 and normalises drag direction with min/max) only ever emits canonical args. The Sell-first panel is Ticket.tsx's Held default (side="sell" with a "Buy more" toggle), not a program constraint.

> *Verifier:* Buying the same range/line twice from one wallet tops up a single LadderPosition PDA (seeded by ladder, owner, lo, hi, h; init_if_needed) — shares and net_paid accumulate, partial sells draw down the same account, and settlement pays shares × level(settled_bin) on the merged total once, then closes the account. Verified on LiteSVM (and already covered by ladder-e2e.test.ts:199-202). The only economic nuance the founder should see surfaced: the second clip is repriced by LMSR on the moved curve, so it costs materially more than the first (2.6× in a 1000-deposit pool for two 100-share tents of reach 4). The "only a Sell button after clicking an owned range" and left-to-right vs right-to-left drawing are frontend state/rendering issues in apps/stook, not program behaviour.

### [high] Void path shorts LPs by every realised trading gain (sell-at-gain before void)

ladder_claim_lp in STATUS_VOID pays lp_share(void_vault − void_basis, deposit, deposit_total), and void_vault − void_basis = deposit_total + Σ_sells(basis_released − sale_amount). Traders are refunded at cost basis, so a position sold at a gain keeps the gain while the buyer who paid for the pump is made whole; the LPs fund the difference. The docstring on the e2e test and architecture.md ('the LP is made whole') is only true when nobody sold at a gain.

> *Verifier:* In STATUS_VOID, claim_lp_handler pays LPs lp_share(void_vault − void_basis) = deposit_total − Σ_sells(sale_proceeds − basis_released), so LPs absorb the net realised P&L of every position sold before the void: sells at a gain reduce the LP refund one-for-one (confirmed on sooth_core.so: A gains 88.012407, B whole, LP claim 911.987593 on a 1000 seed, vault 0), while sells at a loss (including round-trip rounding) over-refund LPs by the same mechanism. The "LP made whole" statement in the e2e test and architecture.md holds only when realised sell P&L nets to zero, e.g. when nobody sold.

> *Verifier:* In STATUS_VOID, an LP tranche receives lp_share(void_vault − void_basis) = its pro-rata share of deposit_total + refunded fees − (net realised P&L of every position closed before the void). Open positions are refunded at cost basis; closed round-trips are left at their traded prices, so LPs lose every realised trading gain AND keep every realised trading loss. 'The LP is made whole' (ladder-e2e.test.ts:282 docstring; not stated in architecture.md) holds only when net realised P&L across closed trades is zero, e.g. when nobody sold. The LP's void-path loss is bounded by Σ realised gains ≤ b·ln(64) ≈ deposit_total (the same LMSR bound as the settle path), so trader refunds are never haircut; reproduced on sooth_core.so: A +88.012407, B 0, LP −88.012407 on a 1000 seed.

### [high] collect_fees is callable while the market is Open: settler bounty can be zeroed and LPs are not made whole in a void

The UI/docs say 'the settler gets half the protocol's cut' and the e2e test asserts 'LP made whole' in a void. Both hold only if nobody called collect_fees first. `collect_fees_handler` (packages/programs-core/programs/sooth-core/src/instructions/ladder.rs:1200-1233) has no status requirement; anyone can drain fees_creator and fees_protocol to their fixed destinations at any time. `settle_bounty(l.fees_protocol)` (line 817) is then 0, and `void_handler` (lines 874-879) folds back only what is left, so the void pot is short by the 20% that already left; traders are refunded first and LPs eat the difference.

### [medium] x-your-stake column, 'best case (Nx)' and the Collect total ignore the transfer fee on the payout leg

The Ticket computes the multiple as `back / q.total` (Ticket.tsx:73) and 'best case' as `q.maxPayout / q.total` (Ticket.tsx:79): numerator is the gross the pool sends, denominator is the net the pool books rather than `pays` (the gross the wallet sends). Collect (Ticket.tsx:112-114, :130) lists and sums gross `shares × level` and `deposit` with no netOf; the button says 'Collect X' but X × (1 − fee) arrives. Sell's 'you paid for these' uses `netPaid` (what the round booked), not what the wallet sent.

## What changed because of it

- `collect_fees` now requires `STATUS_SETTLED` (was callable while open: zeroed the settler's bounty, shorted a void).
- Void refunds are pro-rata across deposits **and** open positions: `claim × void_vault / (deposit_total + basis_total)`. Previously traders were refunded at cost and LPs got the remainder, so sellers' realised gains came entirely out of the house.
- Ticket shows the slippage bound it signs (`at most / at least, if the odds move first`), and every multiple and Collect total is net of the coin's transfer fee.
- Not changed: rounding dust of ~1.5e-11·B per round trip at ≥9 decimals (fee is ~10¹⁰× larger; the 1e-4·D haircut absorbs ~10⁷ such trips); the `.min(lp_pool)` clamp absorbing a unit of tranche rounding on the last claim.
