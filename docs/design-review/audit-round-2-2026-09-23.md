**architecture.** The structure is sound and mostly the right shape for the product: one 64-bin LMSR per round with a global b and tranche attribution is the measured-correct choice, oracle-only settlement with a unique-update rule is right, dropping the order book and adjudicator was right, and the Token-2022 verdict/approval guard is the most defensible piece a DeFi judge will see. What is wrong is not mechanism but parameterisation and framing, and one of those is severe: the ladder opens on a uniform prior over 64 bins and locks 120 s before a close whose price is publicly observable, so the seeder's expected loss on a SPYx/GLDx daily round at the app's hard-coded 1% tier is ~0.99 of the seed (my Gaussian estimate; the blind review's simulations found 60-99% under late sharp flow) and it goes to whoever trades last, i.e. a bot, not the community. The same identity the architecture doc states (E[LP] = -b*KL(final||join)) says the fix is a calibrated prior at create plus a lock gap proportional to the round, which cuts the expected loss to ~10-20% without touching solvency, tranche P&L or the trade path. Second, "canonical daily round" is a client convention (any settles_at >= now+900 is a valid PDA; the calendar keys by NY day and hides duplicates; discovery is getProgramAccounts on a public RPC), which a Series PDA fixes in about two days and also gives per-anchor tier, lock gap and close time a home. Anchors are Crypto.SPYX/USD and Crypto.GLDX/USD (24/7 xStock token feeds), so market hours never block settlement, but a 4pm NY close for ZEC/STONK is arbitrary and weekend stock-anchor rounds settle on thin DEX prints. Biggest judging risk: a judge seeds a round, watches the LP economics as built, and reads "earn 80% of every fee" on the start sheet. Tree left git-clean; an untracked scratch test from another session (packages/sdk-solana/tests/zz-scratch-cu-audit.test.ts) was present before I started and was not touched.

## Findings that survived verification

### Pro-rata void still lets a foreseeable void drain up to ~70% of LP deposits via self-dealing pump-and-dump

*correctness · architecture · reviewer medium, verifiers medium, medium · effort day*

void_share pays every open claim the same fraction void_vault/void_claims, and void_vault is short by every realised gain, so an attacker with two wallets (A buys a bin cheap, P pumps it, A sells into P, P holds into the void) nets exactly gA·D/(D+cP) with all of P's capital refunded pro rata. Across several bins the haircut compounds and the attacker's take approaches D. The 2026-09-23 fix reduced the single-bin loss from 100% of D to ~34%, but did not remove the incentive; it only requires a void to be near-certain before lock (any Equity.US feed created through the SDK on a weekend/holiday — the program accepts any feed_id and has no publishing-hours check; a known Pyth outage at lock; tier 0's 12.5 bps settle-conf ceiling in a volatile hour). Break-even void probability for the measured configs is ~64% because P's bins are far from the price; pumping bin 32 instead turns it into a cheap option.

> **Verifier.** Pro-rata void refunds (void_share = claim*void_vault/void_claims, ladder.rs:876-901, :920, :1123) socialise every realised sell gain across LP deposits and open basis, so a self-dealing pair (A buys cheap, P pumps, A sells into P, P holds into the void) nets exactly gA*D/(D+cP) and LPs lose D*sum(gA)/(D+sum(cP)), bounded by D. Reproduced on HEAD sooth_core.so: D=5,000, one bin x5->12 attacker +1,696.140416 (LP -33.9%); eight bins x4->10 attacker +3,519.889377 (LP -70.4%, cover 0.296); solvency held throughout. The 2026-09-23 fix made the pumper wear its share but did not remove the incentive; architecture.md:110-121 still describes the drain as closed. It is profitable only when a void is near-certain before lock (break-even void probability ~68% for far bins, ~1.6*D to 6*D of attacker capital tied up >24h, 3% fees): the program accepts any feed_id and tier, but the app lists only 24/7 feeds and pins tier 2, so the deterministic vectors (Equity.US on a weekend, tier-0's 12.5 bps ceiling) need an SDK-created round; note a tier-0 round on a coin's anchor feed does show in the app calendar (Coin.tsx:44 filters by mint+feed, not tier). Keeper downtime is not a vector (Hermes historical updates settle within the 24h grace); a Pythnet halt spanning T-120s..T+30s is. Reviewer's Option A (LP-first) zeroes the pair's net only when no other open position exists; with honest open basis cH the pair still nets gA*cH/(cP+cH), i.e. the loss moves from LPs to open traders. Decide and document the void policy (LP-first, mark/unwind refunds, or a feed/tier allowlist in ProtocolConfig) and correct the architecture text.

> **Verifier.** Pro-rata void refunds still let a self-dealing pump-and-dump collect gA·D/(D+cP) from everyone still in (LPs and honest open traders alike) whenever a void can be predicted at lock; the take reaches ~70% of the LP deposit across 8 bins (reproduced: +1,696 on 1 bin, +3,520 on 8 bins, D = 5,000). architecture.md:110-120 wrongly presents the 2026-09-23 pro-rata change as closing this. In-app exposure is narrower than stated: app rounds are 24/7 feeds at tier 2 (50 bps ceiling), settlement is permissionless from Hermes history, and the attacker bears full settle risk on ~6×D of capital (break-even void probability ~60%); the practical foreseeable voids are a hand-run keeper lapsing 24 h (architecture.md:222, no in-app Settle) or a Pyth outage across the 30 s gap at 16:00 ET. The proposed LP-first fix zeroes the attacker only when no honest open positions exist (otherwise it nets gA·cH/(cP+cH) from them, and it pays traders nothing when cash+fees < deposit_total); a mark-to-market void (refund open positions at their sell-back value on the frozen curve, LPs take the residual) removes the payoff for any mix of participants, and hosting the keeper / widening SETTLE_MAX_GAP_SECS removes most of the precondition.

**Proposed fix.** Decide the void policy explicitly. Option A (LP-first): at void set traders' pot = max(0, cash+fees − deposit_total) shared pro rata by net_paid, LPs get min(cash+fees, deposit_total) pro rata by deposit. In every measured scenario this makes the attacker's net exactly 0 (P wears A's gain, which P's purchase price already included) and the LP whole; LPs are only short when cash+fees < deposit_total, which needs a late tranche that joined a distorted curve and was unwound (its settle-path worst case anyway). Option B: keep pro-rata but reduce void foreseeability — refuse feeds that are not 24/7 at create (allowlist by feed_id in ProtocolConfig), and/or widen SETTLE_MAX_GAP_SECS per feed class. Either way state the rule in architecture.md and the Ticket's void copy.

### lp_join has no ceiling on B; a tranche that pushes B_wad past 2^96 freezes every subsequent trade until settle/void

*correctness · bug · reviewer low, verifiers low, low · effort hour*

apply_trade starts with wad_div(delta, b) and wad_div refuses any divisor above MAX_WAD_DIVISOR = 2^96 (≈7.9e10 tokens in WAD). liquidity_for_deposit never divides by b, so join/create happily book a b that large; from then on every buy and sell returns MathOverflow, holders cannot exit, and the market runs to settlement on whatever was open. Reachable in one deposit only at ≤6 decimals (seed ≥ ~3.3e11 tokens, fits u64), or by summing tranches at higher decimals; a griefing vector on a low-value 6-decimal token, not a solvency issue (settle, void, redeem, claim all still work).

> **Verifier.** lp_join/create put no ceiling on B_wad. liquidity_for_deposit never divides by b, so a deposit of >= ~3.295e11 whole quote tokens at a fresh market (less depth per token later, so more needed after trades) books a b above wad_div's MAX_WAD_DIVISOR = 2^96; apply_trade's first operation wad_div(delta, b) then returns MathOverflow for every buy and sell until settle or void. Reachable in one deposit at <= 7 decimals (3.3e18 base units fits u64), via 2 tranches at 8 decimals and ~18 at 9. Confirmed on the built program via LiteSVM: after a 4e17-base-unit join on a 6-decimal mint, B = 1.207 x 2^96, the existing holder's sell and any buy fail with error 6000 MathOverflow, while settle, redeem, claim_lp and collect_fees all succeed and the griefer's deposit is returned in full. Liveness griefing only (no loss to the pool; positions still settle correctly), practical only on a high-supply low-value quote mint. Fix: in join(), require!(new_B_wad <= MAX_WAD_DIVISOR, LadderSeedTooLarge); mirror it in the SDK's liquidityForDeposit / join builder (there is no joinProblem helper today).

> **Verifier.** lp_join/create place no ceiling on B. A tranche (or sum of tranches) that pushes B_wad past 2^96 makes every subsequent ladder_trade fail with MathOverflow (apply_trade's opening wad_div(delta, b)) until the round settles or voids; settle/void/redeem/claim_lp are unaffected and pay correctly, and the freezing tranche itself is refunded in full since no trade can move the curve after it. Reachable only on quote mints with <=7 decimals, requiring > ~3.3e11 whole tokens of total deposits at a fresh curve (more once any bin is a long shot); at >=8 decimals it is unreachable because Ladder.cash/deposit_total are u64 and the crossing join is rejected. Impact is a slot-level denial of service (holders cannot exit or add before settlement) at zero principal loss for anyone; it is the one way a starter can poison a canonical slot, contradicting the "slot cannot be poisoned by its starter" design note. Fix: require!(new_B_wad <= MAX_WAD_DIVISOR) in join(), mirrored in the SDK's liquidityForDeposit.

**Proposed fix.** In join(): require!(new_B_wad <= MAX_WAD_DIVISOR, LadderSeedTooLarge) (and mirror in the SDK's joinProblem/quote so the UI refuses the deposit before sending).

### Terminal dust is stranded forever: no way to close a finished market

*correctness · architecture · reviewer low, verifiers low, low · effort day*

After every claimant is paid a settled market keeps ~1.5 base units per trade in lp_pool (the +1/−1 rounding that never enters tranche_pnl) plus per-tranche fee floors, and a void market keeps up to (claims − 1) units of void_share flooring; the ladder, vault and authority rent stay locked too. Not a payout error (measured: 88–89 units after ~60 trades, 12–16 after 30 void claims) but it accumulates one rent-exempt 1.9 KB account plus a token account per round forever.

> **Verifier.** After every claimant is paid, a settled market retains ~1.5 base units per trade in lp_pool (buy ceil+1 / sell floor−1 rounding that never enters tranche_pnl) plus ≤1 unit per tranche of fee-accumulator flooring (measured: 90 units after 60 trades — lp_pool 89, fees_lp 1), and a void market retains < (claims) units of void_share flooring (measured: 10 after 22 claims). No instruction can reach this residue and no instruction closes the Ladder (1888 B, 14,031,360 lamports) or the vault token account (165 B, 2,039,280 lamports), so ~0.016 SOL of the creator's rent and ~2 KB of ledger state are locked per round forever. The authority PDA holds no lamports and no data, so no rent is locked there. Positions and tranches are closed on redeem/claim, so per-user rent is already reclaimed. This is a known, documented open item (svm-review-2026-09-22 open item 6; architecture.md "nothing sweeps it"), not a payout or solvency defect.

> **Verifier.** A finished market cannot be closed: the Ladder (1,888 B, ~0.014 SOL) and its vault token account (~0.002 SOL) stay rent-locked forever, paid by whoever funded the round first, and a few base units of rounding dust (roughly 1-1.5 per trade in lp_pool/fees_lp, or fewer than one per claim in a void) stay in the vault. The authority PDA holds no rent. Position and tranche rent is returned on redeem/claim, so traders and LPs lose nothing; the cost is ~0.016 SOL per round to the round creator and is already recorded as open item 6 in svm-review-2026-09-22.md and in architecture.md. Fix: two u32 counters in Ladder._reserved, permissionless redeem/claim_lp on behalf of owners so a crank can empty a market, then a permissionless ladder_close that sweeps dust to the treasury and closes the vault and ladder to the creator once counters are zero and fees are collected.

**Proposed fix.** Add open_positions and open_tranches u32 counters to Ladder (incremented on first touch / join, decremented on redeem / claim) and a permissionless ladder_close that, once both are zero and status is final, sweeps the vault remainder to the treasury and closes vault, authority and ladder to the creator.

### Void haircut is attributed by size, not by exposure: a tranche that joined after the realised gain wears the same fraction

*correctness · ux · reviewer low, verifiers low · effort hour*

void_share applies one fraction to every deposit regardless of when it joined, so an LP who joined after a pump-and-dump (and whose tranche was never counterparty to the seller) is haircut identically to the creator; likewise an open position bought after the sale. On the settle path tranche_pnl attributes exactly; on the void path the rule is a blunt average. Consistent and documented in the Ticket copy, but it is the piece of the design a DeFi judge will probe, and the honest 'gain someone cashed out is worn by everyone still in, equally' framing understates that LPs can bear it in full (finding 1).

> **Verifier.** Void refunds attribute the vault shortfall by claim size, not by exposure: void_share (instructions/ladder.rs:896-901) applies one ratio, void_vault/(deposit_total+basis_total), to every deposit and every open position, and claim_lp's void arm (:1123) never reads the tranche's join snapshot, unlike the settle arm's tranche_pnl/acc_fee attribution. Confirmed by ladder-e2e.test.ts:283-334 (lp2 joins after the sale, is haircut at the same ratio) and by a math check: pump under b1, late join, full unwind under b1+b2 nets the trader +35.7 on 2000 of deposits with basis_total=0; the void splits it 17.85/17.85 by deposit, while settle at the likely bin gives LP1 +12.0 and LP2 -47.7. The misattribution runs both ways (early tranches can wear a late tranche's exposure too), and "LPs bear it in full" is reachable only when basis_total is ~0, which requires a join between pump and unwind. Not a solvency or extraction issue (every refund <= what was put in, previously verified); a design/documentation gap. Fix options: document the by-size rule and its timing consequence next to the void rule in architecture.md; show the live void cover ratio in LpPanel before a join (all inputs are already in LadderAccount); or, if exactness is wanted, an acc-style realised-gain accumulator per b-unit snapshotted at join, keeping open positions in the pot so the two-wallet drain stays closed.

**Proposed fix.** If Option A of finding 1 is adopted this mostly disappears (LPs are whole unless a late tranche was unwound). Otherwise, document the attribution choice in architecture.md next to the void rule and surface the per-round void ratio on the LP panel before joining a round that is already trading.

### Canonical slot's grid centre is the creator's choice: opens_at = creation+60s and the keeper opens immediately, so an early-started round is centred days stale and cannot be re-created

*security · architecture · reviewer high, verifiers high · effort day*

The ladder PDA is canonical per (feed, settles_at, mint, tier) and the docstring says 'nothing about a round is the starter's choice' (ladder.rs:168-170), but opens_at is `now + OPEN_DELAY_SECS` at create (ladder.rs:270) and the crank opens any Seeding market as soon as now >= opens_at (crank.ts nextStep). The app itself offers starting rounds 'for the week ahead' (Coin.tsx:2) and the SDK accepts any settles_at >= now+900. A slot started Monday for Friday's close (or a year ahead by anyone with 1 token + ~0.015 SOL) opens within a minute with p0 from that moment; by settlement the price is often bins away, on a 64x1% grid possibly in the tail. Since no trade has happened, the curve is still uniform (1/64 per bin) while the outcome is nearly known, so an LP who joins that open market is sized against a uniform prior and loses up to b·ln64 = 0.9999·D to the first trader who buys the current bin. The slot is then burned for that day: the PDA exists and no one can create the round with a fresh centre.

> **Verifier.** A round's grid centre is read at opens_at = creation + 60 s (instructions/ladder.rs:270; open at :354 only requires the window; the keeper opens immediately via sdk nextStep), while its PDA is canonical per (feed, settles_at, mint, tier) and, once Open, can only settle. So whoever creates the slot first fixes the instant its centre is read, contradicting the :166-170 docstring and the design review's canonical T_open. The app offers starting any day up to ~2 months out at the 1% tier (WallCalendar offset <= 1, StartRound TIER=2), so early-started rounds open with a centre that by settlement may be bins away or off the -27%/+37% grid, and the day's canonical slot cannot be re-created; pre-creating every future slot for every coin costs ~1 token + 0.015 SOL each and is a permanent, unrecoverable grief. The LP loss cited (up to 0.9999·D to a single-bin buyer on an untraded curve; measured: 140 tokens captures 68% of a 2,000-token pool, 1,146 captures 97.7%) is real but is the documented LMSR −b·KL property of any untraded curve near settlement, not specific to early opening; a canonical opens_at bounds staleness to the round length rather than eliminating it, so the LP-side fix must address stale untraded curves (time since last trade, |ln(P/p0)| vs step), not just curve_seq == 0.

**Proposed fix.** Derive opens_at from settles_at like locks_at is (e.g. opens_at = settles_at - ROUND_SECS[tier], or a fixed lead per tier) so a canonical round's centre is always read at a canonical instant regardless of who created it or when; have the keeper open at that instant. Additionally refuse lp_join into an Open market whose curve_seq is 0 (no trade since open) once now - opens_at exceeds some bound, or at least have the app warn when |ln(P_now/p0)| exceeds one step. Fix the docstring claim.

### Collect packs every redeem+claim into one legacy transaction; 15 or more items cannot be sent at all

*svm · bug · reviewer high, verifiers medium, medium · effort hour*

apps/stook/src/components/Ticket.tsx:160 builds a single transaction with [heap, CU limit, CU price, ensureAta, N redeem ixs, M claimLp ixs] and no chunking. Each redeem/claim adds 51 B (32 B unique PDA key + 19 B compact instruction); the base is 492 B, so the 1,232 B legacy limit is crossed at N+M = 15. A wallet with 15+ positions and tranches on one round gets 'Transaction too large' from web3.js before signing and can never Collect from the UI; LP rent stays locked too.

> **Verifier.** Collect (apps/stook/src/components/Ticket.tsx:160) packs every redeem and claimLp into one legacy transaction with no chunking. With the always-present heap/CU-limit/CU-price ixs plus ensureAta the base is 492 B and each redeem/claim adds 51 B, so N+M >= 15 items exceeds 1232 B and web3.js throws "Transaction too large: 1257 > 1232" before the wallet signs; v0 with a 9-key ALT lifts this only to 17 (18 with the user ATA in the table). A wallet holding 15+ distinct shapes (no per-wallet cap; 64 single-bin bands alone are distinct PDAs) cannot Collect positions from the UI in either the settled or void case, since the Ticket shows only Collect after the bell. Tranches are NOT stuck: the House tab (LpPanel.tsx:88) offers a per-tranche Claim. No funds are lost: redeem has no deadline and any SDK caller can send 14-item chunks. Fix: chunk at 14 items per tx in Ticket.tsx (the 14 already accounts for the CU-price ix) and send sequentially; longer term a remaining_accounts `ladder_collect` instruction.

Caveat that decides (b): the push-oracle account is fresh enough only for feeds with a tight heartbeat (prior review measured ~2 min on devnet for crypto, worse for equities); otherwise the client posts via Hermes, which today costs an extra transaction and, while the heap frame exists, cannot share the transaction with the consume (see the allocator finding). *Fix:* Implement (a) first (half a day: optional account in LadderCreate, reuse open's policy block, SDK builder flag, StartRound posts then creates); keep opens_at for the no-price path; add (b) once the allocator is gone so post+trade fit one transaction.

- **[medium/architecture] Q64.64 weights: measured ceiling is ~4% on the everyday tent and ~45% on the rare 64-bin hot band; defer** (svm). With per-bin costs measured at 1,226 (128-bit) and 2,358 (256-bit) CU and wad_mul at ~690 CU, a binary fixed-point weight only removes the division from the reduction: the 4-limb schoolbook product stays (w up to 2^94 x g up to 2^93 still needs 256 bits). Estimated saving: ~250 CU/bin on the fast path, ~1,350 CU/bin on the slow path. Tent (7 bins): 44.5K to ~42.8K. 64-bin hot band: 181K to ~95K. Cost: Rust math + state semantics + the bit-exact TypeScript quote port + every golden test, 2-3 days, with no live markets to migrate. *Fix:* Do the 64-bit-chunk wad_mul (1 h) and the overhead diet first; revisit Q64.64 only if wide plateau shapes on hot markets become a product path, and then as Q32.96 in the same 16-byte slot so account layouts do not change.

- **[info/optimisation] acc_fee at 1e18 scale cannot overflow or saturate; floor loss is sub-base-unit** (svm). accrue_lp_fee adds fee x 1e18 / b_units: fee is a u64 so the product is at most 1.8e37 < 3.4e38 (u128) and never overflows; Sigma fees_lp is bounded by the u64 checked_add on fees_lp, so acc_fee <= 1.8e37 / b_units_min, where b_units_min = 24 base units (one-token seed at 2 decimals), i.e. <= 7.7e35 — the saturating_add never engages. tranche_fees multiplies b_units x grown with b_units <= B_units at every accrual, so the product is <= Sigma fee x 1e18 <= 1.8e37 and checked_mul never fails. Optionally switch FEE_ACC_SCALE to 2^64 (shift instead of a u128 division, ~100 CU) when the overhead diet lands.

- **[medium/architecture] Join guard: replace exact curve_seq with a bounded-deviation check on the curve the LP saw** (architecture). A join fails if any trade landed since the LP read the curve (LadderCurveMoved), which on a busy round means indefinite retries. A tolerance on `sum` alone is not enough: a buy on bin k paired with a sell on bin j leaves S unchanged while distorting both prices, so the sandwich the review measured (+2,802 on 2,500) survives an S-only band. The sufficient statistic is the whole price vector, which fits in the instruction. *Fix:* LadderLpJoinArgs gains `expected_log_w: [u32; 64]` (quantised ln w_i, 256 bytes; fits beside 9 accounts in one tx) and `max_dev_bps`. join() computes the L1 distance between the current and expected price vectors and refuses above the band; extraction is then bounded by ~max_dev * b instead of unbounded. With the Series (finding 3) the product default should be 'fund tomorrow's round', which is Seeding and has no sandwich surface at all; mid-round joins become the exception.

- **[medium/architecture] Settlement liveness: 24 h void is a policy choice that hurts everyone once Hermes history makes a late settle always possible, and a losing trader can trigger it** (architecture). The settle rule picks a unique update, and the keeper fetches it from Hermes' historical route (/v2/updates/price/{settles_at}), which remains available long after T; the Pyth receiver will post a historical VAA. So a settle is possible days later, yet anyone can void after 24 h, and a trader who is losing has an incentive to (void refunds pro-rata at cost). Liveness today rests on one cron keeper with a Pyth API key; the browser cannot self-open or self-settle because Hermes requires the key. The fixed 30 s gap and half-a-step conf ceiling are right for crypto majors but at tier 0 (conf <= 12.5 bps) an off-hours xStock print can fail and void a round that had a perfectly usable price. *Fix:* Raise VOID_GRACE_SECS to 7 days (pro-rata void already removes the LP-drain incentive, so the only cost is lockup); put settle_gap_secs and max_conf_bps on the Series; add the conf-straddle rule (pay two bins pro rata when conf crosses the boundary) rather than voiding; run a second keeper and expose a 'settle this round' button in the app routed through a small proxy that holds the Pyth key so any user can crank for the bounty.
